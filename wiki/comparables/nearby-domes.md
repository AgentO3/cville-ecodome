# Scope

These are existing or documented domes from the [November 23, 2026 research report](/sources/web-research.md). Driving times are that report's approximations from downtown Charlottesville. None of these is an EcoDome facility. The [open question](/questions/open-questions.md) about whether similar domes near Charlottesville can be toured is partly answered here: several can. A 50-foot LED venue with about 250 seats is a narrower match.[^web-research]

The report's practical shortlist for a 50-foot or about-250-person analog is Richmond's Dome, Baltimore's Davis Planetarium, ODU's Lê Theater, and JMU as the nearest high-quality public fulldome. In town, StarLab and occasional Rotunda nights were the only local dome experiences found.

# Other notes from the same pass

The Virginia list on the Worldwide Planetariums Database includes more science-center and school planetariums (Roanoke, Danville, Norfolk State, Arlington's David M. Brown Planetarium, and several Northern Virginia high schools). Seat counts there are mostly under 120. The National Air and Space Museum planetarium page returned HTTP 500 on November 23, 2026, so current tickets were not confirmed.

NSF NRAO has a portable Starlab. NRAO headquarters is in Charlottesville. The published January 3, 2025 debut was at a school in New Mexico, so it is a traveling education asset, not a Charlottesville walk-in theater. IMRSV-XR markets large inflatable 360-degree projection domes from the Washington area. That is a vendor, not a public venue.

The [geometry page](/dome/geometry.md) is EcoDome's own 50-foot figures. This page does not change them.
