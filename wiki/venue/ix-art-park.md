# What the project sources say

The November 2026 [community notes](../sources/community-context.md) associate EcoDome with IX Art Park in Charlottesville, Virginia. They also point at a [digital twin](../prototypes/ix-art-park-twin.md) whose URL and description say it places the dome with satellite imagery and 360-degree views.[^community-context]

The [AI Explorers deck](../sources/ai-explorers-deck.md) does not name IX Art Park. Its path to April starts with "confirm site and delivery responsibilities," and "site access and approvals" is one of the items that unlock the pilot. The structure slide says the venue and festival dates are proposed, and that site and technical configuration are to be confirmed.[^explorers-deck]

Concept renderings in the deck show an outdoor installation. They are labeled as concept visualizations, or as a photograph of a dome with simulated projection. This wiki does not treat those pictures as proof of a chosen site.

# What the park's own site says

Read on 2026-11-23, the [park's home and rentals pages](../sources/ix-art-park-website.md) say the nonprofit Ix Arts Foundation, a 501(c)(3), operates Ix Art Park and The Looking Glass immersive art museum, called there Virginia's first immersive art museum. The outdoor park is a free, year-round mural and sculpture area in central Charlottesville, a few blocks from the downtown mall, and the site of the Market Central Saturday farmers market. The museum and the outdoor park can be rented separately or together. An outdoor rental includes a stage, lighting, and picnic tables. The concrete platform that makes up most of the outdoor rental space is described as about 150 by 100 feet.[^ix-website]

Those pages do not mention EcoDome, a 50-foot dome, or a partnership with this project. Phone, email, and the street address were not copied. A platform of that size is large enough to matter in a later site study. It is not a booking.

# A geodesic dome that is not EcoDome

A [C-VILLE Weekly article](../sources/ix-nest-2016.md) dated August 24, 2016 describes an IX exhibit called Nest, built over a geodesic dome that had been at Burning Man. The article does not mention EcoDome, does not call the dome vegetative, and does not give a diameter. The 2026 park pages above do not mention Nest. Whether that structure is still in the courtyard is an [open question](../questions/open-questions.md). It is not the proposed 50-foot venue.[^nest-2016]
