# Why the study wants a liner

The [feasibility research](../sources/feasibility-research.md) says a picture projected on a geodesic cover shows the frame and fabric wrinkles. It describes a negative-pressure liner: a fan pulls air from the gap between the outer cover and an inner screen so the screen is drawn smooth. It cites SoFlo Studio, Fulldome.pro, DomeGuys, and Pacific Domes for that practice. The exact design is not specified for EcoDome.[^feasibility]

# Version 1 decision

On 2026-11-25, Owen, the project owner, confirmed that the first version of the EcoDome will be a projector-based system. That is the decided approach for version 1. The note is the [owner decision](../sources/owner-decision-v1-projector.md).[^v1-projector]

LED and OLED panels stay a possible later upgrade, including as a candidate for a final build. The November 25 decision does not select them for version 1, and it does not adopt a vendor, a projector count, or a dollar figure. How well projection works on a sunny day is still open. See [open questions](../questions/open-questions.md).

Older wordings stay beside that decision. The [official site](../sources/official-site.md) says LED panel. The [geometry page](geometry.md) keeps the deck's projection package and that LED wording on the record. The estimates below are the study's planning figures for a projection system. They are not a quote, and the decision does not adopt them.

# Projector count

The study says a 50-foot dome is too large for the single-projector fisheye kits used in small inflatable planetariums, which it sizes at up to about 6 meters. It says a 15-meter dome needs several laser projectors, edge-blended and warped. Its planning estimate is **5 to 6 laser projectors in the 10,000 to 16,000 lumen class**. It says the real count, brightness, and lenses must come from a dome projection design, not a brochure.

Reference points it records, not an EcoDome specification: Christie markets 15,750-lumen and 25,300-lumen lasers for mid-sized domes; Zeiss describes multi-channel fulldome layouts in which a "4K" system is roughly 12 to 15 million effective pixels; Blooloop publishes a rule of thumb of about 9 to 10 foot-lamberts for 2D; an Epson Pro L1755U 15,000-lumen rental-grade projector listed at $33,000 and has been seen near $22,400, since replaced by the 16,000-lumen EB-PU2216B, with lenses sold separately.

# Capital tiers

The study says these tiers are planning estimates assembled from benchmarks, to be replaced with integrator quotes.

| Component | Entry estimate | Professional estimate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Projection liner, negative-pressure fan, and curtain | $25,000 | $40,000 |
| Projectors, 5 to 6 lasers, with lenses | $60,000 | $150,000 or more |
| Media server and warp, blend, and calibration software | $8,000 | $25,000 |
| Mounts, truss, and rigging | $5,000 | $15,000 |
| Audio | $8,000 | $25,000 |
| Power distribution, cabling, and cases | $5,000 | $12,000 |
| Lighting, including egress | $3,000 | $10,000 |
| Multimedia subtotal | About $115,000 | About $275,000 or more |

The largest line is the projectors. The study's wider statement is that the multimedia layer is likely **$100,000 to $250,000 or more**, more than the shell. A Fulldome.pro 16-meter package advertised from about £63,000 is recorded only as a signal that integrated packages exist. The page did not make clear whether that figure was rent or purchase.

Other planning figures in the same section, also estimates unless a publisher is named: SoFlo charges $7,000 to climate-control a 50-foot dome, $2,500 a week for a generator, $14,000 to carpet one, and $1,750 a week for a boom lift. Pacific Domes specifies a 10,000-pound reach forklift for a 50-foot install and notes the interior runs about 10 degrees Fahrenheit warmer than outside, with a volume of about 32,700 cubic feet. A truck or trailer is estimated at $20,000 to $60,000, ballast at $3,000 to $8,000, and storage at $800 to $1,500 a month. Virginia guidance, the study says, puts a generator at least 20 feet from the dome. See [permits](../regulations/virginia-permits.md) and [logistics](../operations/logistics-and-crew.md).

Content is described as a recurring cost. One example: Loch Ness Productions prices a one-year license for one show at $2,000 for venues under 5,000 annual attendance and $4,000 under 10,000. Public ticketed music usually needs a performance license. How these costs sit in a year of events is the [profit model](../business/financial-model.md).
