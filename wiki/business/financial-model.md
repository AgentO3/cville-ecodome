# Status of these figures

The [feasibility research](../sources/feasibility-research.md) says every figure in its profit section is a planning estimate for discussion, not a research finding. They are here to show which variables move the result. They are not a forecast, and they are not the [pilot's $175,000 proposal](../pilot/budget-and-sponsorship.md).[^feasibility]

# Mobile projection rentals

Assumptions in the study: average price $20,000 per projection event; direct cost about $9,500 per event; annual fixed costs about $70,000; depreciation about $40,000 a year, from about $200,000 of AV and dome assets over five years.

| Scenario | Events a year | Revenue | After direct costs | Less fixed costs and depreciation | Operating profit before owner pay |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 8 | $160,000 | $84,000 | $110,000 | About −$26,000 |
| Base | 15 | $300,000 | $157,500 | $110,000 | About $47,500 |
| High | 25 | $500,000 | $262,500 | $110,000 | About $152,500 |

The study says the mobile side loses money at about eight projection bookings a year, roughly breaks even to a modest profit around 15, and becomes attractive at 25 or more. Shell-only bookings would add smaller, more frequent revenue on top. The prices behind the $20,000 average are on the [pricing page](pricing.md).

# Seasonal ticketed run

Assumptions: 12 weekends a season, under the [180-day temporary-structure line](../regulations/virginia-permits.md); 3 show days a weekend; 6 shows a day; 120 seats a show, which is 25,920 seats a season; $22 average net ticket; variable costs of 21 percent; season fixed costs about $107,000.

| Occupancy | Tickets sold | Revenue | After variable costs | Less season fixed costs | Season profit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 25 percent | 6,480 | $142,600 | $112,600 | $107,000 | About $5,600 |
| 50 percent | 12,960 | $285,100 | $225,200 | $107,000 | About $118,000 |
| 75 percent | 19,440 | $427,700 | $337,900 | $107,000 | About $231,000 |

The study says this half depends almost entirely on occupancy.

# What the model is used for

- Utilization dominates. Fixed costs and depreciation are large.
- The two halves compete for the same dome. A 12-weekend venue season removes those weekends from rentals, often in peak wedding and festival months. The study suggests putting ticketed shows in shoulder periods.
- A dim or seamed image will not support the higher package prices. See [projection and AV](../dome/projection-and-av.md).
- Starting capital for a professional launch is estimated at **$250,000 to $400,000**, including the dome purchase, the multimedia layer, a vehicle, ballast, and working capital. A leaner path, shell rentals first or rented projectors per event, would cut that and also cut early prices.

This range is not the deck's $175,000 pilot allocation. The [budget page](../pilot/budget-and-sponsorship.md) keeps both. The [hybrid model](hybrid-model.md) is the narrative around the two halves.
